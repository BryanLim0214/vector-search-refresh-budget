What arrives, and how we score it

Blocks are shards. Shaded blocks receive the orange new passages.

Data: 100,000 web passages and 1,000 real search questions from MS MARCO, a public data set. A small language model turns each into 384 numbers that capture its meaning.

Placement: starting center (each passage goes to the shard whose center at the start is closest), one shard (all of a step’s passages on one shard), or eight shards (the closest of eight active shards, so some get more).

Topics: mixed (random order) or new (held back at the start). Budgets of 2, 4 or 8 summaries; 50, 200 or 800 questions per step. Each setting is repeated five times with different random starts.

Data, test settings and scores

About 55 seconds The data are one hundred thousand passages from MS MARCO, a public set of web passages and real search questions. A small language model turns each passage into a list of three hundred eighty-four numbers that capture its meaning. We use a thousand test questions, and keep sixty-four other questions apart for the probe. The animation shows the three placements we test, meaning where a new passage is stored: on the shard whose starting center is closest, all on one shard per time step, or on whichever of eight active shards has the closest topic, so some of the eight get more than others. New passages come either in mixed topic order or from new topics that were held back at the start. Our main score is recent recall. Take a question’s ten true best matches, keep the ones that arrived in the last two steps, and ask what share the system found. Overall recall is the same, counting all ten. Every setting is repeated five times with different random starts. We call a difference clear only when our statistics test says chance alone would rarely produce it, and we give differences in points, where one point is one percentage point. We wrote the plan and the tests down before the main analysis, and the one follow-up we added after seeing results is clearly labeled as added later.
